As shown in the figure, scaling PaLM from 62B to 540B fixes a large portion of the one-step-missing and semantic understanding errors. This suggests that larger models acquire semantic understanding and logical reasoning skills as a function of scale, though we note that the error categories are coarse and some problems exhibit more than one kind of mistake.

We also examined the small number of problems that PaLM 540B got wrong but PaLM 62B got right. These cases were rare and mostly involved arithmetic slips in otherwise sound chains of thought, which the external calculator described in Section 3 would correct. Taken together, these findings suggest that the emergence of chain-of-thought reasoning is driven by improvements in a combination of abilities rather than any single skill.

Why do small models fail? Inspecting the outputs of the smallest models, we found that they frequently fail even on symbol mapping tasks that only require copying from the exemplars, and that they often produce fluent but illogical chains of thought. Smaller models therefore seem to lack the capacity needed to make use of the reasoning patterns shown in the prompt.

A.2 What is the role of prompt engineering?

One of the key considerations of prompting is sensitivity to the exact prompt. There is no shortage of work showing that prompts affect language models in unexpected ways. The general way that we created chain-of-thought annotations was by taking eight exemplars from the training set and decomposing the reasoning process into multiple steps leading to the final answer.

To probe robustness, we had three different annotators write chains of thought for the same exemplars, and also evaluated exemplars written in a deliberately concise style. Although the variance across annotators is non-trivial, all sets of chain-of-thought prompts outperform standard prompting by a large margin, as reported in Table 6 and Figure 6.

We further tested whether the order and number of exemplars matter. Performance was broadly stable across random permutations, and gains from chain-of-thought prompting appeared with as few as one exemplar, increasing modestly as more were added.
